05 Impagliazzo's five worlds · 1995

Five futures, one spectrum.

P vs NP resolves only one bit of the outcome space. The cryptography-relevant structure lives in the average-case regime the question never touches. Walk the spectrum — watch which parts of the digital economy survive at each stop.

WORLD I OF V
Algorithmica

P = NP (or morally equivalent: fast randomized algorithms for NP). All computational assumptions false; key recovery, preimages, collisions all polynomial-time.

Most theorists place us in Cryptomania or Minicrypt — nobody can prove which. P vs NP only separates Algorithmica from the other four; Pessiland is the nightmare: hardness exists but yields no cryptographic advantage.

06 If P = NP — day zero

Set the exponent. Watch it fall.

Suppose a constructive polynomial SAT algorithm lands tomorrow. Whether civilization collapses or merely scrambles depends entirely on c and the constants — an O(N²) solver is "disastrous," an Ω(N⁴) one "poses no immediate practical danger." Drag the exponent and price the apocalypse.

07 The world after

Re-founding security below mathematics.

If computational assumptions fail, security must rest on things that aren't computational assumptions. Five layers survive — strongest first. The literature even anticipated the collapse: Bennett–Brassard–Breidbart showed how to reuse a one-time pad "safely even if P = NP" — in 1982.

Shannon 1949: the OTP is perfectly secret — the proof quantifies over unbounded adversaries, so it survives P = NP by definition. Quantum key distribution anchors security in the no-cloning theorem and no-signalling, not in any conjecture: twin-field QKD over 1,000 km of fiber, the Micius satellite's 7,600 km link, device-independent QKD certified by Bell violations. Wyner wiretap codes and Shamir secret sharing complete the toolkit — and Morimae–Yamakawa (2022) built quantum commitments and signatures without one-way functions.
P = NP is a statement about time, not space. Maurer's 1992 bounded-storage model delivers oblivious transfer and key agreement from a huge public random string plus short keys — provably secure against any adversary with bounded memory, no computational assumption involved. The catch: the honest/adversary storage gap can never exceed quadratic, so this layer degrades as storage gets cheap.
Physically unclonable functions and secure enclaves shift the trust base from mathematics to manufacturing. They don't make inversion impossible — but they move the attack surface from "solve a SAT instance" to "deprocess a chip or suborn a fab": a materially different, slower threat model.
If the collapse algorithm is n^c with large c, protocol design becomes a race to shrink instance sizes: ephemeral sessions, minimal messages, segmentation. This is triage, not security — and it has a formal research program behind it: Ball–Rosen–Sabin–Vasudevan's fine-grained cryptography builds OT, key agreement and signatures whose security is an exponent gap (honest parties n^a, adversaries n^(a+ε)) under SETH/OV/3SUM — assumptions that never mention superpolynomial hardness.
Stored ciphertexts become retroactively breakable, so long-lived secrets demand OTP re-encryption or destruction pre-collapse. Institutional threat models pivot from mathematics to supply chain, side channels, and insider risk — the human layer is the final layer.

08 Fifty years of siege

The siege, year by year.

1955–56
Nash & Gödel's letters
Nash to the NSA: key computation length grows exponentially — "I cannot prove it." Gödel to von Neumann: deciding questions in K·n² time "would have consequences of the greatest magnitude."
1971
Cook's theorem
SAT is NP-hard; the question is posed. Levin independently formulates it (published 1973).
1972
Karp's 21 problems
Hamiltonian cycle, TSP, Clique, Subset Sum, k-Colorability — all reduce to SAT. One question absorbs them all.
1975
Barrier I — Relativization
Baker–Gill–Solovay: oracles A and B with P^A = NP^A and P^B ≠ NP^B. Diagonalization is blind here.
1985
Haken + Razborov
Resolution needs 2^Ω(n) clauses on pigeonhole. Razborov: clique needs n^Ω(log n) monotone gates. Hope crests.
1994
Barrier II — Natural Proofs
Razborov–Rudich: every known lower-bound technique would, if it worked, break every PRF. Lower bounds ≈ codebreaking.
1995
The five worlds
Impagliazzo splits the outcome space: Algorithmica, Heuristica, Pessiland, Minicrypt, Cryptomania.
2000
The Clay wager
$1,000,000 on the answer. Fifty years on: no poly algorithm for any NP-complete problem, no superpolynomial lower bound for any explicit function.
2008
Barrier III — Algebrization
Aaronson–Wigderson: even arithmetization-based techniques face vetoing oracle worlds. The toolbox is now formally empty.
2011
Williams' algorithmic method
Faster Circuit-SAT for ACC⁰ ⇒ NEXP ⊄ ACC⁰ — the first new general-circuit lower bound in decades, by inverting the pipeline.
2015
Fine-grained harvest begins
Backurs–Indyk: truly subquadratic edit distance refutes SETH. The Wagner–Fischer n² DP is conditionally optimal; quadratic walls fall like dominoes.
2016
5n − o(n) · fine-grained crypto
Find–Golovnev–Hirsch–Kulikov push general circuit lower bounds to 5n. Ball et al. build the first cryptography priced in exponent gaps.
2020
Liu–Pass theorem
One-way functions exist ⟺ time-bounded Kolmogorov complexity is mildly hard on average. Meta-complexity and cryptography fuse.
2023–24
The wall holds
Ilango: SAT reduces to MCSP given a random oracle. Fine-grained sequential functions (ASIACRYPT 2024). No ε anywhere on the horizon.
